Toolkit

The tools I use every week, sorted by the job they do.

what I use

A tool only makes this list after I’ve made something real with it. Where I can, I link to the thing I made.

  • think & build

    Claude

    My thinking partner and builder. Drafts, research, the code for this site, and the scripts behind every video on it.

    Made with it: everything on the home page

  • think & build · plan B

    If Claude goes down

    I don’t rely on one AI. I’ve tested a backup, so my work carries on the day my main tool doesn’t. Most small teams have never asked what they’d do on that day.

    The backup models I’ve looked at are in my research log.

    Wondering how you’d set one up for your team? Message me on LinkedIn.

    Status: tested, not yet needed.

  • remember

    claude-obsidian

    Runs my personal knowledge vault in Obsidian. Articles and notes go in; Claude files them into linked pages I can ask questions of later.

  • remember

    Open Notebook

    An open-source take on NotebookLM that I run on my own Mac, for documents I’d rather not upload anywhere.

  • email

    Superhuman Mail

    Where I read and answer my Gmail. I joined in November 2020, reached inbox zero within days, and my inbox has been clear at the end of every day since. Split inbox, snooze and follow-up reminders mean nothing slips.

    Using it since: November 2020

  • make music & video

    ChatCut

    A video editor you drive with prompts. My first stop for songs and for short AI video clips.

    Made with it: Home Is You · Same Sky · Carve Her a Heart

  • make music

    Mureka

    Song generator, in English and Mandarin. I use it inside ChatCut, or on its own as a backup. It gives a timing for every sung word, which makes lyric videos much easier.

    Made with it: Home Is You · Same Sky

  • make music

    ElevenLabs

    For music that has to fit an exact length, or when I need clear licence terms.

  • make video

    OpenArt

    Many image and video models in one place, so I can try a few side by side before choosing one.

    Made with it: Light Left On

  • make video

    Google Flow

    Google’s studio for AI images and short video clips. I make most of the pictures in my music videos here, and use a cast sheet so the same faces appear from scene to scene.

    Made with it: Iron Rice Bowl · Beijing, Winter · Still Searching

  • make video

    HyperFrames

    Builds a video from a web page. Claude writes each scene as code and HyperFrames turns it into an MP4, so I can change a title, a lyric or a cut by asking for it in a sentence.

    Made with it: Light Left On · One Question at a Time · Models as Insider Risks

for my teaching

Tools I plan to use in the classroom. Not on the list above yet, because I haven’t made something real with them.

  • work together live

    CodeShare

    A free shared text editor that needs no sign-up. Open a page, share the link, and everyone types in the same document at once. Handy for learners to write and compare a prompt together.

  • collect ideas

    Padlet

    An online board where learners post text, images and links on one shared page. Good for gathering everyone’s ideas or questions in one place during a session.

where I learn

Learning sites run by the AI companies. I’ve listed what each one says it offers; I haven’t reviewed the courses.

  • learn Claude

    Claude Academy

    Anthropic’s own learning site, and it says it’s free. Short courses on using Claude and AI well: AI Fluency, what AI can and can’t do, and working in teams of people and AI agents (still in beta).

  • learn AI at work

    OpenAI Academy

    OpenAI’s learning site for people at work, from beginners to people building with the API. Courses on using AI at work, building with AI tools, leading AI adoption and teaching with AI, plus livestreams and replays. The page doesn’t say whether it’s free.
