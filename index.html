How these are made

Every video here starts with something real: a year I lived through, or a question I kept hearing at work. I write down what happened, shape the words with Claude, and check every line against what took place. AI tools sing the songs and draw the scenes. Then I pick the takes, cut and fix them until they say what I meant.

Why three copies? The same page runs in three places, each for a reason.

  • GitHub Pages: the main copy. Free, static, and opens on almost any network.
  • Cloudflare Pages ↗: adds what a static page can't do: live A&E waiting times and a wider news search, fetched by a small server. Some office networks block pages.dev, so use the GitHub copy there.
  • Manus ↗: an experiment. I asked the Manus AI agent to rebuild the page from the public code, to see how close it gets on its own. It adds Manus's own visitor analytics, and it may go offline.

Start with the problem, not the AI

Singapore is rolling out three new AI programmes for smaller businesses, with up to 12,000 places. CNA spoke to Johnson Poh, Assistant Chief Executive at IMDA, about what SMEs should do with them. Nine minutes, and most of it is advice an owner can use on Monday.

Why it’s worth an SME owner’s time:

start
with what eats your team’s time. Sometimes the fix is simpler than AI and works just as well.
data first
scattered documents and spreadsheets are the usual blocker. One of the programmes (with Singtel) turns them into data AI tools can use.
try first
IMDA keeps a list of pre-approved solutions you can test before committing, and wants most of them AI-ready within two years.
skills too
the programmes come with training and help to put tools in place, not just access to them.
the test
is the business better off? Time saved, customers served faster, costs down, revenue up. Using AI because everyone else does isn’t a reason.

Nearly one in four Singapore SMEs now use AI, up from one in seven a year earlier. If you’re not one of them yet, this is a calm place to start.

The alarm nobody hears

Three years of AI alarms. “AI will take your job.” “Entry-level is dead.” “This changes everything.”

Janan Ganesh wrote in the Financial Times about “pessimism fatigue”: when warnings keep not coming true, people stop listening, and then miss the one that matters. AI talk at work has the same problem. Doom and hype wear people out the same way.

The warnings that still get heard are specific:

what
invoice matching
who
your industry
when
the next 12 months
watch for
a competitor quoting in hours, not days

You can check a warning like that. If it’s wrong, you’ll know, and so will I.

about

The timeline below covers over two decades in learning and development. The subject kept changing: 3G phones for dealers, every iPhone launch for a call centre, 3D laser scanners for police and air-crash investigators, then learning design and academic quality on campuses. The job never did: take something new and make it work for people who didn’t ask for it.

Today I’m a Workplace Learning Manager, working with Singapore companies on how their people learn at work. Where I work, and who backs me, are on LinkedIn. In 2025 I took a year out to retrain in AI automation, and in photography and video with AI. I don’t write code, but I build with AI: the live dashboards on this page, explainer videos, a few automations.

When I sit down with an SME owner, I start with one question: where does your team’s time go? Then we pick one job, fix it with the simplest tool that works, sometimes AI and sometimes not, and train people on the real work until they can use it without help.

I don’t do this alone. When a problem needs more than training, I can bring in specialists, from engineering and IT to business and design, plus applied-innovation teams who solve problems for industry.

If your team still needs me a month later, I haven’t done my job. Run a team and not sure where to start? Message me on LinkedIn. There’s more support for this kind of work than most owners realise.
