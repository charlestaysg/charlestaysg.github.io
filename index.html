Vulcan Post · Q4 hiring outlook
Read on Vulcan Post ↗
24 Oct 2026 · worth knowing · jobs

Q4 hiring: flat, and harder to get in

About 1 in 3 Singapore employers plan to add staff from October to December, and about 1 in 5 plan to cut. ManpowerGroup’s survey nets that out to +13%: the same as last quarter, and down from +20% a year ago. Job worries are at their highest since Blackbox Research started asking in 2023, with about 1 in 5 people naming jobs as a top concern.

Trade and logistics leads the hiring plans. Finance and insurance improved the most, and UOB says it will take in 1,000 fresh graduates over the next year. The information sector, which covers software and media, fell furthest.

Two things I’d keep in mind. The survey counts plans, not hires. And the slowdown didn’t start this quarter; it arrived by mid-year and has held. Resident unemployment is still around 3%. What has changed is how long it takes to get in: fresh graduates and people who lost a job are waiting months longer than before. The article puts much of the software slowdown down to AI. That’s the writer’s reading, and a fair one, but the survey doesn’t measure it.

UOB’s move is the part I’d point an SME owner to. A big employer decided entry-level jobs are still worth having when AI takes over the routine tasks. Someone still has to learn the work well enough to check what the AI produces.

What a small business can do:

hire now
good people are waiting longer for offers. If you’ve been putting off a hire, this quarter gives you more choice than last year did.
try first
a short paid task tells you more about a candidate than a polished CV.
keep the junior role
if AI now does the routine part, give the junior the checking. They learn the work by checking it.
offer learning
you may not match a bank’s pay. You can offer real work early and someone to learn it from.

CNA · back to work after 50

23 Oct 2026 · worth reading · CNA

Train older staff while they still work for you

CNA followed workers in their 50s who lost their jobs. In April to June, about 4 in 10 of them found work within six months, down from about 5 in 10 the quarter before. Vacancies still outnumber jobseekers, so the jobs are there. Getting hired is the hard part.

The part I’d point an SME owner to: Singapore research found that older workers who learned on the job had better job outcomes than those who relied on formal courses. Contacts, and work other people have seen them do, count for more than certificates. One man in the story retrained, sent over 400 applications and got no interviews.

What a small business can do:

learn at work
give staff over 50 a real new task and someone to learn it from, rather than only sending them on a course.
change the job
routine work gives people no reason to learn. Add one part to the job that needs a new skill.
write it down
keep a short record of what each person can now do, so the skill is easy to check later.
hire across
an older worker from another industry can bring skills you need. The woman in the story moved from garments to medical-parts manufacturing through a career conversion programme and now works as a data analyst.

Two of the three people CNA spoke to got back in through someone they knew, not a job ad.

18 Oct 2026 · worth knowing · online security

An ad that can hack a phone without a click

Researchers at Citizen Lab found spyware that reached Al Jazeera staff through online ads in 2024 and 2025. Nobody had to click: the ad only had to load on a web page or in an app. A company posing as an ad seller bought ad space and aimed the ads at chosen people by location and internet address. Once in, the spyware could copy passwords, documents and messages, and take screenshots. Google has since cut the ad company off its platform.

What a small business can do:

update
turn on automatic updates for phones and browsers. This attack needed a flaw in the browser or app, and updates close those flaws.
restart
restart phones once a week. Some spyware doesn’t survive a restart.
fewer ads
an ad blocker on work laptops means your devices open fewer ads.
the few at risk
staff who handle payments, contracts or personal data can turn on Lockdown Mode (iPhone) or Advanced Protection (Android).

Each batch of targets cost millions, so most SME staff won’t face this exact attack. Cheaper versions of the same trick, called malvertising, are common.
