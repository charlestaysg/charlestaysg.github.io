Learning on the job

Most of what people know about their work, they picked up at work. Here is how a new hire goes from watching to working alone, in six stages. Pick a workplace and step through it.

the short version

  • An expert’s skill is mostly invisible. They make dozens of small calls without saying why, so a new hire copies the moves and misses the reasons.
  • Cognitive apprenticeship is a way to make that thinking visible, then hand the work over step by step.
  • At the start the coach carries most of the work. By the end the learner carries almost all of it. The handover is the whole point.

    if you train people at work

    TipWhy
    Think out loudThe new hire can see your hands, not your head. Say what you check and why, even when it feels obvious.
    Take supports away on purposeA checklist that never goes away becomes the job. Set a date to drop it.
    Ask why, not only whatIf they can explain their choice, they can handle the next case that looks a bit different.
    Skip what they already knowSomeone with experience doesn’t need to watch you from the start. Ask them to do the task first, then decide where to begin.
    End with something newWorking alone on the case you showed them is not the finish line. Handling one you never showed them is.
