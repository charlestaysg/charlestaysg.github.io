her story

Xia Jinhe grew up in Lujiang County, Anhui, raised by her grandmother. Online she was 奇迹夏夏, Miracle Xiaxia.

In 2021, at 18, she fainted from pain in her right leg in the middle of the gaokao maths paper. She came round and finished the paper. The pain was osteosarcoma, a bone cancer. Her offer from a teachers’ college reached her in a hospital ward. She never got to use it.

By her own account, she went through most of the treatment that followed without her parents. At 19, on Qixi, the Chinese Valentine’s Day, her right leg was amputated. She signed the consent form herself. “A leg for a life,” she said. “Worth it.” Over five years she had six or seven major operations and 25 rounds of chemotherapy, and she signed her own forms every time.

She saved for her own treatment and her prosthetic leg. When she was well enough, she went home and ran a street stall on that leg, selling bead bracelets and crocheted dolls she made herself. People offered to start a crowdfunding page for her. She said no: everyone works hard for their money.

Nurses in Shanghai remember her arriving alone, pulling a suitcase, smiling. Her profile gave her age as 100, the age she hoped to reach. She liked to say that once life has dragged you onto its pirate ship, you may as well be a happy pirate.

Her last wishes were small: to see the sea, and to buy her grandmother new clothes. In a message typed for after she was gone, she thanked everyone, said sorry for breaking the deal and for being a deserter, and asked people to understand her pain. “The world stays online,” she wrote. “I’m logging off first.” She asked to be buried where there are trees, with flowers and grass beside her.

She died in Shanghai on 27 October 2026, at 8:28 in the evening. She was 23. In the comments on her account, thousands of strangers left the same words: 夏夏，晚安. Goodnight, Xiaxia.

I read about her on 30 October and kept thinking about the empty chair beside her bed. I never knew her. The song is sung by a stranger, the way all those strangers said goodnight. It follows her life in order and ends where she asked to be: somewhere with trees.

她的故事

夏瑾禾在安徽庐江长大，是奶奶把她带大的。在网上，她叫“奇迹夏夏”。

2021年，18岁的她在高考数学考场上，因为右腿剧痛晕倒。醒来以后，她把卷子写完了。那份疼，是骨肉瘤。师范学校的录取通知书寄到了病房，她再也没能用上。

据她自己说，之后的治疗，大多是她一个人面对的。19岁那年的七夕，她的右腿被截肢，手术同意书是她自己签的。“一条腿换一条命，很值。”五年里，她做了六七次大手术、二十五轮化疗，每一次都是自己签字。

她自己攒钱治病、装假肢。身体好一点，就回到家乡，装着假肢摆摊，卖自己串的珠子和钩的娃娃。有人提出替她发起筹款，她谢绝了：大家赚钱都不容易。

上海的护士记得，她第一次来的时候，一个人拖着行李箱，笑着走进来。她把主页上的年龄填成一百岁，那是她想活到的年纪。她常说：“既然上了生活的贼船，何不做一个快乐的海盗呢。”

她最后的心愿很小：想去看海，想给奶奶买新衣服。在提前写好的告别信里，她谢谢所有人，为“违约”道歉，说自己“当了逃兵”，请大家理解她的痛。“这个世界online，我先下线了哦。”她希望自己葬在有树木的地方，旁边有花草相伴。

2026年10月27日晚上8点28分，她在上海离世，年仅23岁。在她账号的评论区里，成千上万的陌生人留下同一句话：夏夏，晚安。

10月30日，我读到她的故事，一直放不下她病床边那把空椅子。我不认识她。这首歌由一个陌生人来唱，就像那些对她说晚安的陌生人一样。歌按她走过的路一段段唱下去，最后停在她想去的地方：有树的地方。

how it was made

I kept two rules. No picture of her, and no voice that pretends to be hers. The video shows only the things and places from her story: the exam desk, the consent form, the stall, the suitcase, the sea, the trees. Her parents are not in the song; only her side has been reported.

the plan
Claude: reading the news reports, the shape of the song, the lyrics and the checks
the song
ChatCut: a male baritone with piano, slow and low until the last chorus
the pictures
Google Flow and ComfyUI (Qwen-Image, on my own Mac): 25 watercolours
the video
HyperFrames: built as web pages, rendered to MP4, subtitles timed to her lines
