Six months of teaching myself AI

The questions I chased on my own laptop, what worked, what didn’t, and what each one means for a small business.

the short version

  • I don’t write code. Since April 2026 I have learned AI by picking a real question each month and trying it myself, mostly with free tools.
  • I check claims before I repeat them, including claims from AI.
  • Some things didn’t work, and two are still unfinished. They are on this page too.

Run a small business? The for a business lines are for you, and there’s a first-month plan at the end.

question by question

  • Apr 2026
    the starting point

    Which laptop do I need to learn this?

    tried
    Bought one, tested it with my real work for three days, and returned it for a bigger one.
    learned
    Measure with your own work before you trust any estimate, AI’s included.
    for a business
    Buy inside the return window and test before you commit. The full story: the test that overruled AI.
  • May
    private answers

    Can my own notes answer questions with no internet?

    tried
    Loaded my notes into a free app that runs a small AI model on the laptop, with nothing sent online.
    learned
    Simple lookups worked. Questions that joined two facts got confident, wrong answers.
    for a business
    A cheap chatbot over your company files can sound right and be wrong. Test it on questions you already know the answer to.
  • Jun
    build it smaller

    Can I make a simpler version myself?

    tried
    With AI’s help, built a small offline tool: it finds the five most relevant notes, then asks a mid-size model to answer from them only.
    learned
    Good enough for “what did we agree?” questions. The answer is only as good as the notes it finds.
    for a business
    Tidy, well-named documents matter more than the AI on top of them.
  • Jun
    check the claim

    Can a laptop really run a 200-billion-parameter model?

    tried
    A post going round said yes, and listed even bigger models. I checked each claim against real download sizes.
    learned
    Half true. One model of that size fits, squeezed hard. The bigger ones in the post don’t fit at all.
    for a business
    Viral AI claims are often partly right. Ask which part, before you spend money on it.
  • Jul
    what stays private

    Which work should never leave my laptop?

    tried
    Set up one app that can switch between online AI models and models running on my own machine.
    learned
    My rule now: general thinking goes to online models, which are better at it. Anything with names, figures or confidential details goes to the model on my laptop.
    for a business
    You don’t have to choose between useful AI and data protection. Split the work by how sensitive it is.
  • Aug
    free alternatives

    Is there a free, private version of the tools I pay for?

    tried
    Installed an open-source alternative to Google’s NotebookLM and pointed it at models on my laptop. Also a free tool that turns web pages and documents into clean text for AI.
    learned
    It works, with more setup than the paid version.
    for a business
    Free and private is possible. Budget time for setup, or someone who can do it.
  • Oct
    new skills

    Can I make music, pictures and 3D?

    tried
    Had AI write music as a score that free instrument sounds play. Drafted songs with ACE-Step, a singing model that runs on the laptop. Made painted stills for Light Left On with ComfyUI, about half a minute each. Installed Blender and connected it to Claude, to start learning 3D. Tried Fish Audio, an online tool, for AI voices.
    learned
    Local tools are good for drafts. For the finished song I still use an online tool, because it sings with more feeling.
    for a business
    Draft in-house for free, and pay only for the final version you put in front of customers.

how the learning works

  • start from a real question

    Not “learn AI”, but “can my notes answer this without the internet?”

  • try the free version first

    Most of the tools here are free. I pay when a free tool falls short.

  • check, don’t trust

    Posts, sales pitches and AI answers get the same question: does it hold up when I try it?

  • write it down

    Every attempt gets a short note: what I tried, what happened, what I would do next. That is where this page came from.

  • say what didn’t work

    The wrong answers and unfinished builds taught me as much as the ones that worked.

try it in your business

You don’t need a powerful laptop or any coding to start. What carries over is the method: one real question at a time, free tools first, check the answers, write down what happened. Here is a first month done the same way.

  • 1 · pick one task

    Start from a weekly annoyance

    Replying to quotation requests, writing a job ad, summarising a supplier contract. One real task, not “learn AI”.

  • 2 · sort your information

    Two piles, before you type anything

    Safe to share: public information and general questions. Never goes in: customer names, prices, payroll, NRIC numbers, anything under NDA. Use only the safe pile for the first month.

    Someone senior in the company should know the PDPA well and keep that knowledge up to date. Every organisation is already required to appoint a Data Protection Officer. A one-day course like PDPA for Everyone is a practical place to start. For whoever holds the DPO job, the three-day Practitioner Certificate in Personal Data Protection goes further, into the day-to-day work of protecting data.

  • 3 · try it free

    One well-known assistant, two weeks

    Use the free version on that one task only. Don’t buy anything yet.

  • 4 · test it

    Five questions you already know

    Ask it five things you know the answer to and count the wrong answers. That number tells you how far to trust it.

  • 5 · write it down

    A one-page log

    What you asked, what came back, time saved, mistakes. Ten lines a week is enough.

  • 6 · decide

    Keep, pay or drop

    At the end of the month, decide. Pay only when the free version falls short, and use trials you can cancel.

  • 7 · next task

    Then pick the next one

    When a task needs the private pile, look at business plans that protect your data, or a model that runs on your own computer. That is the point to get help setting it up.

Two more things. Ask your staff first: some of them are probably using AI already and know where it helps. And expect some failures. Write them down; they show you the limits faster than the successes do.
