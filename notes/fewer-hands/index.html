Fewer hands, no first rung

Four things are squeezing hiring in small firms at the same time. None of them is going away, and each has a fix that costs more effort than money.

Checked 25 Oct 2026. Manpower rules change often; check MOM before you act on them.

the short version

pressurewhat changedwhat you can do
fewer young localsBirths keep falling, so fewer school leavers join each yearHire mid-career, and train for the job instead of hiring for it
older staff stay longerThe re-employment age went up to 69 in July 2026Write down what your seniors know before they leave
foreign hires are a top-upLevies, quotas and pay floors keep moving upPlan headcount on what you can keep, not on what you can add

one by one

  • fewer young locals

    The pool gets smaller every year

    Singapore’s resident fertility rate fell to 0.87 in 2025, a new low. The government has said the citizen population could start to shrink by the early 2040s. For a small firm, that means fewer school leavers and fresh graduates, and the bigger firms reach them first.

    widen the net
    mid-career switchers and people coming back to work after a break are a bigger pool than school leavers, and easy to overlook.
    train in-house
    hire for attitude and basic skill, then teach the job on the job. A written training plan for the first 90 days does most of the work.
  • older staff stay longer

    Your most experienced people are still here, for now

    On 1 July 2026 the retirement age rose to 64 and the re-employment age to 69. Both go up again, to 65 and 70, by 2030. In many small firms the person who knows how everything works is already in their sixties.

    redesign the job
    less lifting and fewer night shifts, more checking, coaching and the jobs only they can do.
    capture it now
    record them walking through a job and turn it into a one-page checklist. How to do this cheaply →
  • safe in physical jobs

    Have you checked these?

    Many SME jobs are physical: lifting, standing all day, working in the heat. If you are taking on mid-career or older staff for them, answer these honestly before day one.

    the job
    does your job ad describe the physical task (“carries 20 kg cartons through the shift”), or does it ask for an age? Do you ask every candidate the same question about it? Have you tried a paid trial shift? TAFEP’s hiring guidelines ↗
    the risks
    when did you last look at your risk assessment? Does it cover the loads, the standing and the heat for an older worker? Every employer needs one, reviewed at least every three years. How to do one ↗
    the kit
    are there trolleys or lifting aids for the heavy items, or is everything carried by hand? Could the loads be made lighter?
    the heat
    do outdoor or warehouse staff get water, shade and breaks on hot afternoons, and does anyone check? Heat check for today →
    the cover
    is every employee doing manual work covered by work injury compensation insurance? MOM’s page ↗
    the log
    if someone hurt their back tomorrow, could you show who was trained on that task, and when? Do you write down near misses, or only injuries?

    Any “not sure” is where to start. If the work carries real risk, ask a workplace safety consultant to walk the floor with you.

  • foreign hires are a top-up

    Not a way round the first two

    Work Permit and S Pass holders come with a levy and a sector quota. Employment Passes are scored under COMPASS. The Local Qualifying Salary, which decides which local staff count towards your quota, went up again in July 2026. The rules are built so that foreign hires add to a local team rather than replace it, and they get tighter over time.

    check first
    look at your quota balance on MOM’s online services before you promise anyone a job.
    spend on the team
    money that would have gone on one more pass often does more as training or a better tool for the people you have.
  • mixed teams

    Onboarding doesn’t happen by itself

    Many SMEs mix local staff and work-pass holders. A team like that works well when everyone learns the job the same way and is held to the same standard.

    buddy
    pair every new hire with one named person for the first month.
    plain SOPs
    short steps, photos, and safety briefings in the languages your staff read.
    same rules
    write down how you hire, pay and promote. The Workplace Fairness Act was passed in 2025 and is expected to take effect by the end of 2027. Until then, the Tripartite Guidelines on Fair Employment Practices apply.
  • no first rung

    Where do juniors learn now?

    Juniors used to learn by doing the simple, repetitive tasks, and AI is good at exactly those tasks. In May 2026, Singtel’s Singapore CEO said a fresh graduate’s job there is now “the equivalent of someone with maybe two years of work experience”. Big firms can absorb that jump. A small firm that hands every simple task to AI will end up with no one ready to step up in five years.

    keep some tasks
    let juniors do a share of the routine work themselves, even when AI could do it faster.
    check together
    when juniors use AI, a senior reviews the output with them and explains what is wrong and why.
    real jobs
    put juniors on real customer work early, with someone beside them. How coaching on the job works →

when a post says it’s all collapsing

There is a lot of fear online right now about ageing, jobs and AI. Plenty of it opens with real numbers and then jumps to conclusions the numbers don’t support. Three checks before you change a plan because of something you watched:

  • Are the numbers real? Look up one of them yourself.
  • Does the conclusion follow from the numbers, or does it go well past them?
  • What does the person telling you this get if you believe them: views, sign-ups, a sale?

The four pressures above are real and slow. They reward steady work on hiring and training more than any big move.
