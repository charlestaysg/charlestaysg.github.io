How Gen AI tools rated my AI skills

I asked two AI tools to rate my website against Singapore’s AI skills scales, then had a third check their work. Here is how they worked it out, what they found, and where they got it wrong.

Scanned by Gen AI tools. Everything on this page came from AI tools reading public web pages. No person or official assessor rated me. The levels are AI judgements, not test results or certificates, and the tools can be wrong. Official scores come only from the tests themselves.

what I asked

I wanted to know if this site shows a clear level of AI skill, measured against Singapore’s own scales. Perplexity and Gemini got the same question, word for word:

Read my website and its linked pages. Search for Singapore’s AI skills levels and competency scales. Look at what each level requires. Based only on the evidence on the website, what level do I show? Name the scales, map evidence to each skill, give an overall level, list the gaps, and say what would move me up one level. Be candid, not flattering.

Then Claude Opus checked both answers, and Claude Sonnet wrote up the result.

how the tools worked it out

  • 1 · same question

    Two tools, identical wording

    Perplexity (GPT-6.1 Sol, Search mode) and Gemini (Flash).

  • 2 · read the pages

    Only what is written down

    Each opened the home page and some linked pages: the toolkit, “Six Months of Teaching Myself AI”, “Twenty Years of Trying Tools”, the Skills Training Landscape and “Testing AI Voices”. They did not run my tools or look at how they are built.

  • 3 · find the scales

    Search for the official pages

    Perplexity drew on 40 web sources, mostly IDG, IMDA, SIT and SkillsFuture pages. Each tool picked the scales it thought fit, so their choices differed.

  • 4 · match

    Evidence against level descriptions

    Each scale says what a person at each level can do. IMDA’s Basic level for building AI apps means a Gen AI app that looks up information before it answers. My offline notes tool finds the five most relevant notes and answers only from them, so that counts as Basic. There is no points score. The tool judges how well the evidence fits the wording.

  • 5 · check

    A third model checks the first two

    Claude Opus read the same pages, opened the official scale pages, and tested each claim against the site. It flagged what was wrong, made up or rated too high.

  • 6 · write up

    A fourth model writes the summary

    Claude Sonnet combined the three answers, with no new research.

  • 7 · check the checker

    Back to the source

    Opus said IDG has three pathways. It had only looked at IDG’s home page. The pathways page lists four starting points, so that correction stayed out of the result.

What the site shows best

  • The “Six Months” page: my notes chatbot gave confident wrong answers, so I rebuilt it smaller. I checked a viral claim against real download sizes and listed the work I haven’t finished.
  • Four live tools for SMEs, each built around one real user’s job. Built with AI, though they don’t use AI when they run.
  • A clear split between private material, kept on local models, and safe material sent to online tools, turned into a first-month plan for small firms.

What’s missing

  • No measured result: no time saved, no before and after.
  • No proof the method works for someone else. No SME or learner case yet.
  • No published test of my own tools against questions I know the answers to.

The tools also caught a mistake on this site. My AI Readiness Index line was out of date: it stopped at “AI Competent”, said 15 minutes and left out the personal version. I fixed it the same day.

what to take from this

  • These are judgements, not scores. Ask again and the answer may move up or down a level.
  • Tools see only what is written down. A skill I have but haven’t shown here counts for nothing. Hence the same gap kept coming up: no measured result.
  • One answer wasn’t independent. Gemini hung on three new chats and only replied inside an older chat that already held its earlier verdict. Perplexity’s fresh answer was more reliable.
  • Every layer made mistakes, including the checker. A second model caught most of them. Checking the source pages myself caught the rest.
  • For a real score, take the tests: SIT’s AI Ready Quiz, AI Singapore’s pAIRI, and the IDG Build 301 capstone.
