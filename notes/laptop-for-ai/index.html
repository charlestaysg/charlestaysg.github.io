The test that overruled AI

How my question kept changing while I chose a laptop, and what finally settled it.

how to read this

The laptop matters less here than the thinking. Over five weeks in March and April 2026 my question changed three times, from “which laptop is light enough?” to “how much memory does AI need?” to “what does my real work use?”. I bought one machine, tested it and returned it before I settled.

AI’s advice was wrong twice along the way. Neither time was it a bad answer to the question I asked. The first time my question changed. The second time my own test numbers showed its estimate was too low.

Each step shows what I asked AI, what I did myself and what I decided. The looking back lines come from going over the chats afterwards with AI, including where the final choice is weakest.

decision by decision

  • 8 Mar 2026
    start from the need

    Do I need a Pro at all?

    asked AI
    To compare the MacBook Air and Pro for writing, everyday work and carrying around. Heavy work already ran on my desktop PC at home.
    decided
    No Pro. A light laptop next to the home PC.
    looking back
    A good answer to that question. The question was about to change.
  • mid-Mar
    the question changes

    What if the laptop runs AI itself?

    did myself
    Wrote a plan to learn to run AI models on my own machine as well as in the cloud. A laptop question became a memory question.
    asked AI
    Which matters more for running models locally: more memory or more graphics cores?
    decided
    Memory first. The M5 Pro with 64GB over the M5 Max with 48GB.
    looking back
    Held up. Memory decides which models fit at all. The Max with 48GB couldn’t hold the larger models I had in mind.
  • mid-Mar
    how much is enough?

    Is 64GB enough?

    asked AI
    Whether my plan needed 128GB. Three phases: tidy my documents with AI, then photo and video editing, then train small and mid-size models on my own material.
    decided
    AI said none of the three needed 128GB. I ordered the 64GB Pro.
    looking back
    That was an estimate, not a measurement. I didn’t see the difference then. One thing I did right without planning it: I bought inside the return window.
  • 23–26 Mar
    test with real work

    What does my real day use?

    did myself
    Stopped guessing and measured. Ran my normal work all at once over three days: an AI model running locally in LM Studio, plus Claude, Chrome, Word and PowerPoint. Screenshotted Activity Monitor as memory climbed.
    decided
    58GB of 64GB used, and the Mac had started swapping to disk. For a machine I wanted to keep five years, that was too little room.
    looking back
    The Mac wasn’t struggling. Memory pressure stayed green the whole time. The call was about where models are heading over five years, not about that week.
  • 26 Mar, 5am
    change course

    Upgrade, or live with it?

    asked AI
    Took the test numbers back to AI. With real data in front of it, the advice changed to 128GB.
    decided
    Changed to the 128GB model and returned the 64GB one inside the window.
    looking back
    The weak spot: 128GB is more than my written plan needs. The case rests on room to grow, not on work that needs it today. I may have over-bought, and I would rather say so here than pretend otherwise.
  • 13 Apr
    check what arrived

    Is this one a good unit?

    did myself
    Ran the same benchmark five times on collection day to check the results were consistent, before the return window closed.
    decided
    Kept it, with a plan to use it about five years.
  • Oct 2026
    what changes

    What I do differently now

    For the next big purchase I buy inside the return window and test with my own work before I commit. A few days of measuring taught me more than weeks of comparing spec sheets, AI’s estimates included. And when AI’s advice changes, I first check whether my question changed.

what carries over

This was a laptop, but the same habits work for new AI tools, new topics and decisions at work.

  • say what it’s for

    AI answers the question you ask. When my use changed from writing to running models, the right answer changed with it.

  • estimate, then measure

    An estimate is a starting point. My own numbers from a few nights of real work settled it.

  • keep a way back

    Buying inside the return window turned an expensive guess into a cheap test.

  • name the weak spot

    The case for 128GB rests on the future, not on today. Writing that down keeps me honest about it.

The options I weighed, and why
  • kept

    M5 Max, 128GB

    Room to grow. More than my plan needs today, as noted above.

  • bought, tested, returned

    M5 Pro, 64GB

    Cooler and longer on battery. Fine for my work that week, too tight for five years once I measured it.

  • ruled out

    M5 Max, 48GB

    Not enough memory for the larger models. Memory mattered more than speed.

  • first idea

    A light laptop

    Right for writing and carrying around. Overtaken once the plan included running AI locally.

One figure to check yourself: my chats quoted two different memory speeds for the M5 Max. Apple’s spec page is the one to trust.
