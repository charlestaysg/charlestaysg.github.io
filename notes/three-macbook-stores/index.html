The education discount wasn’t the cheapest

Apple sells MacBooks in Singapore through three online stores. I matched the same laptop across all three, and my rule of thumb didn’t survive.

what i assumed

I had a ranking in my head for years. Education pricing is the best deal Apple offers, the regular store is full price, and refurbished is for people who don’t mind a used machine. If you qualify for the Education Store, you go there.

I never checked it. So I opened the three stores side by side and matched the same chip, memory and storage in each.

Refurbished was cheapest on all three. On the MacBook Pro it beat education pricing by S$270. The education discount on these models is S$130 to S$150. Refurbished takes about 15% off the Air and Pro, and anyone can buy it.

why the belief stuck

  • the gate

    You have to prove you qualify for education pricing. A gate makes a deal feel bigger than it is.

  • the word

    “Refurbished” sounded like “used”. Apple’s refurbished Macs carry the same one-year warranty and 14-day returns as new ones. The M5 MacBook Air I looked at first came out in March 2026.

  • the old check

    I compared once, years ago, and stopped. Prices, models and base specs all changed. My conclusion didn’t.

how to compare

  • 1 · same spec

    Compare the spec, not the model name

    “MacBook Pro from S$2,699” and “refurbished MacBook Pro S$1,939” look like a S$760 saving. They’re different machines: 1TB against 512GB. The fair match is the refurbished 1TB model at S$2,289. Line up the chip and its core counts, memory, storage, screen size and extras before you compare prices.

  • 2 · starting price

    Watch what “from” means

    New 14-inch MacBook Pros now start at 1TB. Apple doesn’t sell a 512GB one new any more, but the refurbished store has one at S$1,939. When the base model moves up a tier, the “from” price rises and the discount looks the same.

  • 3 · extras

    Count only what you would use

    The Education Store lets you choose your own specs and adds free engraving, up to 10% off AppleCare+ and a cheaper Creator Studio plan. Each July to September it runs a back-to-school offer. In 2026 that took S$149 off AirPods or AirTags bought with a MacBook Air or Pro. That’s a saving only if you were buying them anyway.

  • 4 · trade-offs

    Know what refurbished gives up

    You pick from what’s in stock that day. No custom builds, no engraving, and refurbished Macs were left out of the back-to-school offer. If you need one exact spec in one colour, buy new.

  • 5 · whose label

    “Refurbished” depends on who sells it

    The warranty and returns above are Apple’s own. A third-party shop’s “refurbished” carries whatever terms that shop sets. Check the warranty, who services it and the return window.

the habit to keep

Rules of thumb go out of date quietly, and you keep following them. This one took three browser tabs and ten minutes to check. I’d gone years without opening them.

Before a big purchase: put the options side by side, match the specs, and let the numbers decide. Then check again next time, because the numbers will have moved.
