From AirPods to tubes

How I went from AirPods Pro to two Astell&Kern players and a tube amp on my desk, and why an hour of listening now does more for my head than my phone does.

where it started

For years my music was AirPods Pro and Spotify. Fine for the bus. Then I started editing video and needed to hear what was really in the mix, so I bought closed studio headphones, the Beyerdynamic DT770 Pro.

On my first visit to AV One, the staff added a FiiO QX13, a small USB-C DAC/amp, so my iPhone could play lossless files properly. The song was Spanish Harlem. I’d known it for years, and that day I heard parts of it I’d never noticed.

why it matters to me

It started in late 2025. I was taking a SkillsFuture Career Transition Programme course in video and trying out audio tools, so I was already listening closely.

When I sit down with a player and my earphones, it’s the high-resolution recordings that get me. Songs I thought I knew sweep me off my feet.

It’s both the music and the ritual: picking the player, tubes or no tubes, the cable. And it has changed how I switch off after work.

If you’re stressed and thinking this is just an expensive hobby, my advice is simple. Find the player and the headphones or in-ear monitors that take the songs you already love to the next level.

the two players

Both are Astell&Kern A&ultima players. Astell&Kern is a Korean brand. Most of its rivals at this level are Chinese: FiiO, iBasso, HiBy, Cayin and Shanling.

What I heard

On paper the SP4000 is the better player. In the shop, with my own headphones as the fixed reference, I picked the SP3000T with its tubes on. Six months later I added the SP4000. Now I choose by mood: the tubes when I want to unwind, the SP4000 when I want detail.

what sets them apart from the Chinese flagships

This section should be fair. The Chinese brands make excellent players, and on several counts they win.

Where the Chinese flagships lead

  • Power for the price. FiiO’s M27 claims at least 5,000 mW per channel into 32 ohms from its balanced output in its highest gain mode, at a much lower price.
  • Flexibility. iBasso’s DX340 lets you swap amp modules, including one with tubes.
  • Same chips, sometimes. Shanling’s M9 Plus uses the same AKM AK4499EX and AK4191 chips as A&K. So the chip list alone doesn’t explain the difference.

What Astell&Kern does differently

  • Real vintage tubes in the flagship, switchable. The SP3000T uses two military-spec Raytheon tubes. You can run tubes, op-amps or a mix, and change it per song.
  • One modulator for every DAC chip. The SP4000 pairs four AK4191 modulators with four AK4499EX DACs, one to one.
  • The body is part of the sound design. Silver-plated steel, copper, 904L steel and ceramic. A&K says the metal shields the circuits from noise. I can’t measure that. I can tell you they feel like instruments, not phones.
  • Its own tuning. A&K’s TERATON ALPHA circuit and its tuning give its players a house sound. You either like it or you don’t, so listen first.

What you pay extra for: materials, the tube design and A&K’s tuning. Not more power, and not more features.

the rest of the chain

My Woo Audio journey started in November 2026. At my desk, the SP3000T now plays into a Woo Audio WA7 Fireflies (3rd gen), a tube headphone amp and DAC in a small glass cube. I set the player to line out, so it sends a fixed-level signal and leaves the volume and the final amplification to the WA7. The WA7 then drives a HiFiMan Arya Unveiled, an open-back planar magnetic headphone.

The first song I played on it was Spanish Harlem again, the same song from my first visit to AV One. The sound was amazing. This time I heard bass I’d never noticed.

The Arya Unveiled is rated at 27 ohms and 94 dB/mW. Reviewers say it plays from most sources but opens up with a strong amp. For me, the WA7 is that amp. Woo Audio rates it at up to 1.5 W per channel from its 4.4 mm balanced output, for headphones from 8 to 600 ohms. Its two 12AU7 tubes can be swapped. On the SP3000T, switching to the tubes gives the same songs a different flavour.

HiFiMan is a Chinese brand and Woo Audio is American, so my setup mixes brands anyway.

Out and about it’s the 64 Audio U18s in-ear monitors with a Toxic Cables Medusa 2 cable. The DT770 Pro still does video editing.
