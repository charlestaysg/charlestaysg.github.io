Before you buy anything

What an SME really needs to make its files and know-how usable by AI. Mostly habits, and tools you already pay for.

Checked 24 Oct 2026. Software plans change; check before you rely on them.

the short version

A post went round this week saying the biggest barrier to AI in companies is messy knowledge, information and data. I half agree. Messy files do stop AI being useful after the first week. But in the SMEs I work with, the files are rarely the first problem, and a new system is almost never the fix.

This is what I would put in front of a 20- to 80-person company:

layerwhat most SMEs needcost
storageThe Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 you already pay foralready paid
housekeepingOne naming rule, one owner per folder, a clear-out every quarterstaff time
asking questionsNotebookLM (in Workspace) or Copilot (in Microsoft 365)included, or a per-user add-on
captureA phone recorder, meeting transcripts, one-page checklistsclose to zero
privacyA restricted folder and a written rule on what AI never seesstaff time
judgementYour best people training juniors on real jobssupervisor time

Four of the six rows are habits. Only one is a purchase most firms haven’t already made.

check which problem you have

The Singapore numbers point in different directions. IMDA counted 14.5% of SMEs using AI in their business in 2024. A study commissioned by AWS found skills shortage was the top blocker, named by 43% of firms. EY put cost and not knowing which tools fit side by side.

So ask one question before you organise anything: has anyone here tried to get AI to answer a question from our own documents, and failed because the documents were the problem? If nobody has tried yet, the gap is skills and habits. A data clean-up won’t close it.

layer by layer

  • storage

    Use the drive you already have

    Don’t add Notion, Confluence and a document system on top of it. Three places to look for a file is worse than one messy place.

  • housekeeping

    Three rules, no software

    folders
    one shared drive per team, not per person.
    names
    a file name rule people will remember: date, client, type of document.
    owner
    one named person per folder who clears out old versions every quarter.

    A lot of paid “knowledge organisation” time ends up here. Try it in-house first.

  • asking questions

    Comes bundled

    google
    NotebookLM is included in Workspace Business Standard and above. Make one notebook per team; answers point to the page they came from.
    microsoft
    Copilot does the same over SharePoint, as a paid add-on per user.
    sensitive files
    an open-source tool like Open Notebook can run on one office computer with a local model, so nothing leaves the building. Slower, and someone technical has to set it up.

    Test it with ten real questions your supervisors get asked every week. When an answer comes back wrong, the fix is usually the document, not the tool.

  • capture

    The step people skip

    What matters most in an SME often isn’t in a folder. It’s with the technician who has been there 18 years, or the owner who knows which supplier is always late.

    Capturing it is cheap. Record the senior person talking through one job as they do it, get a transcript, and turn it into a one-page checklist. Put the checklist in the shared drive.

  • privacy

    Before the first upload

    Customer IC numbers, staff medical certificates and salary files often sit in the same drives as the SOPs. Before you point any AI tool at a folder, decide what is off limits under the PDPA and move it out. A separate restricted folder is enough. Write the rule down before something leaks into a chat, not after.

  • judgement

    Where files run out

    Writers on expertise from Michael Polanyi to Gary Klein make the same point: much of what an expert knows can be written down, but the judgement calls that set them apart from a newcomer mostly can’t. A notebook will hold the checklist. It won’t hold the feel for when the checklist is wrong. That still needs a junior working beside the expert and getting corrected, on real jobs.

what you can skip

  • A data lake or data warehouse. That’s for firms whose product is data.
  • A custom chatbot from a vendor, until the bundled tool has failed on your real questions.
  • A “knowledge platform” subscription that copies the drive you already have.
  • A long readiness study before anyone has tried anything.

where outside help earns its fee

IMDA’s CTO-as-a-Service gives SMEs a free first consultation on which tools fit, and pre-approved AI tools can be co-funded under the Productivity Solutions Grant. Use those before paying anyone for software advice.

Paid help makes sense on the people side: deciding whose job changes, getting your experts to pass on what they know, and training staff to check AI answers instead of trusting them. No tool does that part for you.
